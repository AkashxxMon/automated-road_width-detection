Rail-Road Crossing Detection

Finds every place a road crosses a railway alignment, measures each road's width from satellite imagery, and decides the structure for each: a box culvert, a bridge with its span along the track, or no structure because the road can be re-routed.

Overview

No level crossings are permitted on new lines, so every road the alignment cuts needs a grade-separated structure or a diversion. The tool runs three steps, one after another:

StepWhat it doesScript
Find crossingsLoads the road network around the alignment (NIC road data, plus OpenStreetMap where NIC has none), finds every point where a road crosses the line, its chainage and crossing angle, the road class, and whether the road is present on both sides (rules R1-R11).crossing_identify.py
Measure widthsFor each crossing, fetches a satellite image crop, segments the road with SAM3, measures its width on 5 lines across it, reads the full road corridor (carriageway | median | carriageway), and judges paved or unpaved.measure_width_sam3.py
Decide structuresMerges crossings that are one road corridor, then decides and sizes the structure for each (rules S0-S6, R9, R12).structure_decide.py

Re-running only Decide structures takes seconds, so thresholds can be tried freely once the widths are measured.

Quick start

  1. Alignment: keep the default DFCC line or upload your own (GeoJSON, KML/KMZ, GeoPackage, zipped shapefile). For a CAD export, pick the alignment layer in "Use only these lines" and check the dashed preview lands in the right place; if not, choose another UTM zone.
  2. Enter the start chainage of the line, in metres.
  3. Area and run name: clip to a state or use the whole line; give the run a name.
  4. Adjust any rule (every input shows a short explanation when you hover over it), tick the steps to run, press Run.
  5. Watch the crossings appear on the map, the SAM3 crops in SAM3 results, and the decisions in the Crossings table. Rows highlighted in orange need a person to look at them.
  6. Download the results from Downloads. Use Open a finished run to look at an earlier run again without re-running it.

Use the Hide button at the top of the inputs panel for more room; Inputs on the map brings it back.

Detection rules (R)

RuleConditionResult
R1The road is present on both sides of the alignment.Crossing - needs a structure decision.
R2The road ends at the alignment (one side only).No crossing.
R5Two crossings within 30 m sharing a route number.One crossing (divided road).
R6A road junction within 50 m.Flag NEAR_JUNCTION.
R8Another crossing within 40 m on the same piece of line.Flag for review.
R9Measured roads within 20 m edge to edge and roughly parallel.One structure; the others MERGED.

R12Minor road within 100 m of a higher-ranked crossing.MERGED, diverted to it.

R3 and R4 were retired: NIC road data is a merge of state exports with gaps between them, so a road that is not joined to the wider network is a data gap, not a missing road.

Structure rules (S)

The first rule that matches sets the structure; flags add up and never change it.

RuleConditionResult
S0No crossing (R2, R10, R11).NONE
S1Minor road narrower than 4 m, within 500 m of a major-road crossing, or with no measured width (width-and-spacing rule); or unpaved (surface rule).NIL_REROUTE - re-routed to the nearest structure. Otherwise the minor road is kept and sized like a major one, with the minor clearance.
S2Kept road with no measured width.PENDING_SURVEY
S4More than one road (divided road, or 2+ roads in the measured layout).DUAL_DESIGNER_DECISION: one box over the whole width, a twin-cell box and a bridge are all worked out; the first that fits is suggested.
S5One road, opening fits a standard box (RDSO DFC loading table, config/box_sizes.csv).BOX - smallest standard size clearing the required height. A box is square to the road, so no span.
S6One road, opening wider than the largest standard box.BRIDGE, span along the track = opening ÷ sin(crossing angle).

The crossing angle (and skew = 90° − angle) is reported for every crossing, so there is no separate high-skew flag; a bridge's span always includes the skew.

Chainage and crossing IDs

  • Chainage is measured along the alignment line in the direction it was drawn: its first vertex is the start chainage you enter, and chainage increases towards its last vertex. If the line was digitised the other way round, reverse it in your GIS before uploading.
  • Clipping to a state does not restart chainage: it is measured from the start of the whole line, so the same crossing has the same chainage whether you run one state or the whole line.
  • With several lines (sections) in the file, each starts at the start chainage.
  • Chainage is written to 3 decimals (millimetres) and shown as km+metres, e.g. 12+345.678.
  • The map shows chainage marks every 100 m when zoomed in (from street level), every 500 m, 1 km or 5 km further out.
  • Crossing IDs are numbered in chainage order: the number in the ID increases with chainage along each line, lines in the order they appear in the file.

Output table

The Crossings tab under the map lists every crossing found, with the decision once the structures step has run.

  • Sort: click a column heading (click again to reverse).
  • Find on the map: right-click a row and choose Zoom to it on the map (double-click does the same).
  • Find in the table: right-click a crossing on the map and choose Show in table, or use show in table in its popup.
  • Show, hide and reorder columns: Columns opens the list - tick to show, drag to reorder. You can also drag a column heading sideways. Reset restores the default set. Your choice is remembered in this browser.
  • Only those needing review shows just the highlighted rows.

Map

  • Crossings are coloured by structure (legend at the bottom right) once decided; before that, green = measured, grey = not yet measured or no crossing.
  • Click a crossing for its details and its SAM3 QA crop.
  • The layer control (top right) switches to satellite imagery and toggles the alignment, chainage marks, crossings, structures and the measured width lines.

Basis and validation

  • No level crossings on new lines - Railway Board directive (August 2023): every road the line cuts gets a grade-separated structure or a diversion.
  • Road classes follow the IRC hierarchy (Expressway, NH, SH, MDR, ODR, Village road), read from the NIC road data's class columns or OSM tags. The class decides major vs minor and flags implausible widths; it never sets the design width.
  • Widths come only from SAM3 (Meta's Segment Anything 3, promptable concept segmentation) on satellite imagery - measured on 5 lines across the road; a SUSPECT measurement is labelled, never replaced.
  • Box sizes are the standard sizes in the RDSO "DFC Loading" box culvert tables (single cell 1-6 m, twin cell 4-6 m), in config/box_sizes.csv.
  • Thresholds checked against the designers' structures in Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh and West Bengal: designers built at 4% of crossings under 15° (R10), 19% of tracks (R11), 16-23% of crossings within 100 m of another (R12), 24% of village roads within 500 m of a major-road crossing against 66% beyond 1 km (S1). The width-and-spacing rule raised decision accuracy from 0.63 to 0.69 over the surface rule.

Limitations

  • Roads missing from both NIC and OSM are missed crossings.
  • Imagery date, tree cover and shadow affect SAM3 widths; the SUSPECT flag catches some of this.
  • Results support feasibility and design review; they do not replace a field survey.
